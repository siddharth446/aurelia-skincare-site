Sample rituals

Three steps, twice, and then stop thinking about it.

Skin routines fail when they're quizzes. The concept ships two — one for waking up, one for winding down — and nothing else unless you go looking.

A.M. — First Light ritual

  1. Melt — thirty seconds on dry skin, water after. Nothing squeaks.
  2. First Light — four drops, pressed in not rubbed.
  3. Crème Veil — a pea, and SPF over it, always, this is the whole sermon.

≈ 2 min

P.M. — Night Shift ritual

  1. Melt — a second pass if the day wore makeup.
  2. Night Shift — two drops, twice a week at first. Skin keeps a calendar too.
  3. Crème Veil — a little more than morning. You're not going anywhere.

≈ 3 min

Before you ask

Questions, answered without marketing gloss

Can I actually order Aurelia?

No — Aurelia is a studio concept build. The products, prices, and founder quote are invented to demonstrate e-commerce design craft. The “Add — sample” buttons count items for the demo but nothing ships.

Are the bottle illustrations real product renders?

They're real CSS. Each bottle, jar, tube and mist is built from divs, gradients and border-radius — zoom in and the edges are still vector-sharp because they are literally geometry, not pixels.

Why so few products?

Because a concept gets to ignore assortment pressure. Six SKUs is the argument: a shelf you can read in ten seconds beats a catalog of duplicates at different prices.

Do the ingredient percentages mean anything?

They're plausible figures chosen for the copy design — niacinamide and azelaic at commonly used cosmetic ranges. No formula exists to test, so treat them as typography with numbers in it.

What's the shipping and returns policy?

For a concept: imaginary. The design decision it lets us show is the boring trust furniture — free-threshold bar, 30-day language, and a bag that behaves — done tastefully instead of shoutily.
